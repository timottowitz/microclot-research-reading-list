# Microclot Research Reading List

This is a reading list of microclots research papers: primary studies, one Cochrane review, critiques and replies, and official guidance. Each entry gives the study type and size, what it found and the main caveat. It is not medical advice, not a complete bibliography and not a ranking of who is right. Microclots have no validated clinical test and no proven treatment.

## 1. What microclots are and the first findings

- **Pretorius et al., 2016.** *Journal of the Royal Society Interface.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2016.0539). Lab study using healthy donor blood and plasma. Tiny amounts of bacterial lipopolysaccharide changed fibrin fibres into an amyloid-like form. Caveat: done on healthy donor samples in the lab, before the pandemic. Discussed in: [Resia Pretorius and the key microclot papers](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/resia-pretorius-microclots/).
- **Pretorius et al., 2020.** *Cardiovascular Diabetology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-020-01165-7). Preliminary report on 20 people with COVID-19, 10 with type 2 diabetes and 10 healthy controls. Microclots were seen in native plasma and were higher in COVID-19 than in type 2 diabetes. Caveat: small and preliminary. Discussed in: [Resia Pretorius and the key microclot papers](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/resia-pretorius-microclots/).
- **Pretorius et al., 2021.** *Cardiovascular Diabetology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-021-01359-7). Small laboratory study of 11 Long COVID patients, 13 healthy volunteers, 15 acute COVID-19 patients and 10 people with type 2 diabetes. Plasma from Long COVID patients held large anomalous deposits that resisted breakdown. Caveat: small groups from one lab, using microscopy and proteomics. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Kell, Laubscher and Pretorius, 2022.** *Biochemical Journal.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/BCJ20220016). Review and hypothesis paper. It argued that fibrin amyloid particles could block capillaries and underpin many Long COVID symptoms, and cited a preliminary report on triple anticoagulant therapy. Caveat: a hypothesis, not a trial. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Kruger et al., 2022.** *Cardiovascular Diabetology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-022-01623-4). Proteomics study of 99 Long COVID patients and 29 controls. More molecules were caught inside the microclots, including von Willebrand factor, platelet factor 4 and antibodies. Caveat: its point that routine markers such as CRP can miss the trapped molecules is a hypothesis, not proof that normal routine results hide microclots. Discussed in: [The amyloid fibrin test](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/amyloid-fibrin-test/).

## 2. Testing methods

- **Pretorius et al., 2022.** *Cardiovascular Diabetology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12933-022-01579-5). Observational study that used a grading system for microclots and platelet pathology and reported significant microclots in all 80 Long COVID samples analysed. Caveat: grading by eye, and the same group later wrote that its microscopy lacked statistical strength, objectivity and throughput. Discussed in: [How to test for microclots](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/how-to-test-for-microclots/).
- **Turner et al., 2023.** *Heliyon.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e19605). Observational study of 40 Long COVID patients and 20 healthy controls using imaging flow cytometry. Microclots were higher in Long COVID, and the method added objective counting to what had been microscope grading. Caveat: small cohort, mainly from one research network. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Automated microscopic measurement of fibrinaloid microclots, 2024.** *bioRxiv (preprint).* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.06.588397). Preprint describing software that measures microclots automatically from microscope images. Caveat: not peer reviewed. It points toward less judgment and more counting. Discussed in: [How to test for microclots](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/how-to-test-for-microclots/).
- **Schofield et al., 2024.** *Blood Advances.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2023012473). Study of 104 critically ill ICU patients and 20 healthy controls. Microclots appeared in 44 patients (42.3%) and in none of the controls, and high counts were linked to disseminated intravascular coagulation and 28-day mortality. Caveat: a single ICU cohort, not Long COVID, and the odds ratio had a very wide confidence interval. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Thierry et al., 2025.** *Journal of Medical Virology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/jmv.70613). Observational study of 50 Long COVID patients and 38 healthy controls. Microclot counts and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) markers were higher in patients. Caveat: small cohorts with overlapping research teams, and healthy people also showed some particles. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).

## 3. The critics and the debate

- **Fox et al., 2023 (Cochrane review).** *Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD015775). Systematic review that appraised five laboratory studies and found the particles in people with post-COVID condition, healthy people and people with diabetes. Three studies relied on images without quantifying particle amount or size, and the risk-of-bias assessment showed concerns about conduct and reporting. It judged the evidence insufficient to link the particles to the condition, found no randomised trials of plasmapheresis, and said plasmapheresis should not be used for this outside a well-conducted trial. Caveat: it did not say the particles are an artefact, and it argued that "microclots" is the wrong word. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Hunt et al., 2024.** *Research and Practice in Thrombosis and Haemostasis.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rpth.2024.102442). Editorial by haematologists, several of them Cochrane authors. It argued that the inferences for the thrombosis hypothesis of post-COVID condition are not based on evidence, that unlicensed antithrombotic drugs are not justified and that apheresis should be reserved for well-designed trials. The authors also raised a safety concern: publicity has sent patients toward apheresis and triple anticoagulant therapy, which carry known adverse effects and no proven benefit. Caveat: an opinion piece, and the researchers replied that it repeated points they had already rebutted. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Kell, Khan and Pretorius, 2024.** *Research and Practice in Thrombosis and Haemostasis.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rpth.2024.102566). Reply letter to the Hunt critique. The authors said the critics repeated points they had already rebutted and that several critics were also authors of the Cochrane review they relied on. They pointed to a decade of work, over 60 joint publications, fibrin found inside the particles and the ICU study's prognostic value, and invited the critics to work together on the nature of the particles and the terminology. Caveat: a letter, not new data, from the group whose work is being criticised. Neither side says the particles are invented, and the disagreement is over whether the evidence is enough to act on. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **Reported particles are not blood clots, so anticoagulant drugs are not a plausible treatment, 2024.** *Research and Practice in Thrombosis and Haemostasis.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rpth.2024.102598). Letter that repeated three concerns: no individual sample data in the early papers, no published inter-assay variability, and no statistical comparison of patients with healthy controls. Caveat: a letter, and the other side disputes its reading. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).
- **No evidence of hypercoagulability, altered fibrinolysis, or increased fibrin(ogen) microaggregates in never-hospitalized long COVID, 2026.** *HemaSphere.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/hem3.70501). Independent UK study of never-hospitalised Long COVID (55 samples) that found no difference from recovered controls. Caveat: one study, in never-hospitalised patients, so it does not settle the question for other groups. Discussed in: [Are microclots real?](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/are-microclots-real/).

## 4. Spike protein and amyloid fibrin

- **Grobbelaar et al., 2021.** *Bioscience Reports.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1042/BSR20210611). In vitro study. Recombinant spike S1 added to normal plasma induced fibrin(ogen) changes tied to resistance to fibrinolysis, with amyloid-sensitive staining. Caveat: lab dish work, which suggests live virus is not needed for a spike-related fibrin effect but does not show it happens in people. Discussed in: [Microclots and the COVID vaccine](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/post-vaccination-microclots-fibrinolysis/).
- **Nyström and Hammarström, 2022.** *Journal of the American Chemical Society.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c03925). A Linköping team scanned the spike sequence and found seven stretches likely to form amyloid, three of which met every amyloid criterion in the lab. Caveat: lab work on spike segments, not patient samples. Discussed in: [Long COVID blood test](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/long-covid-blood-test/).
- **Westman et al., 2025.** *Biochemistry.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biochem.5c00550). Purified-system study of seven spike-derived amyloid peptides. Spike685 gave the strongest fibrinolysis resistance, and Spike601 impaired fibrin formation by sequestering fibrinogen. Caveat: no patient samples and no treatment tested, so it does not show that every Long COVID patient has this pathology. Discussed in: [Microclots and the COVID vaccine](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/post-vaccination-microclots-fibrinolysis/).
- **Waters et al., 2026.** *Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2026.1753348). Mass spectrometry study of microclot deposits from 14 people with persistent post-vaccination symptoms and 16 healthy controls. It reported amyloid-positive microclot material and coagulation protein changes. Caveat: unrecognised prior infection could not be ruled out, so a vaccine-specific cause is unproven. Discussed in: [Microclots and the COVID vaccine](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/post-vaccination-microclots-fibrinolysis/).
- **Bhattacharjee et al., 2025.** *medRxiv (preprint).* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.18.25322379). Decentralised cross-sectional study of 42 people reporting chronic illness after vaccination and 22 controls. It found different immune profiles, more recent Epstein-Barr virus reactivation and higher circulating spike protein. Caveat: a preprint, cross-sectional, and the authors call for further investigation rather than a diagnostic signature. Discussed in: [Microclots and the COVID vaccine](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/post-vaccination-microclots-fibrinolysis/).

## 5. Treatment studies

- **Triple anticoagulant therapy, 2023.** *Research Square (preprint).* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-2697680/v1). Uncontrolled preprint of 91 patients given clopidogrel, aspirin and apixaban, with microclots stained before and after. Reported symptom and microclot improvement. Caveat: no control group and not peer reviewed, so improvement cannot be pinned on the drugs, and bleeding is the known risk. Discussed in: [Microclots treatment](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-treatment/).
- **Clinical improvement of Long-COVID is associated with reduction in autoantibodies, lipids, and inflammation following therapeutic apheresis, 2023.** *Molecular Psychiatry.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-023-02084-1). Uncontrolled biomarker study from Dresden. In patients who improved, fibrinogen fell about 70% and fibrin fibres largely disappeared on dark-field microscopy. Caveat: no control group, so it ties a blood pattern to improvement without proving the procedure caused it. Discussed in: [Microclots treatment](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-treatment/).
- **Plasma exchange therapy for the post COVID-19 condition, 2025.** *Nature Communications.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57198-7). Randomised, double-blind, sham-controlled phase II trial in 50 people. Plasma exchange was safe but no better than sham. Caveat: this is plasma exchange, not DFPP or H.E.L.P., so it does not settle those methods. Discussed in: [Microclots treatment](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-treatment/).
- **Jaeger et al., 2022.** *Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2022.1007636). Perspective paper proposing H.E.L.P. apheresis for severe long COVID. Caveat: not a trial result, and two authors declared a patent on that use. Discussed in: [Plasmapheresis for long COVID](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/plasmapheresis-long-covid/).
- **A single-dose of oral nattokinase potentiates thrombolysis and anti-coagulation profiles, 2015.** *Scientific Reports.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep11601). A single oral dose in 12 healthy men changed D-dimer and clotting times within normal ranges. Caveat: healthy volunteers, with no microclot measurement. Discussed in: [Microclots treatment](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-treatment/).
- **Rasouli, Hartl and Konecky, 2025.** *Frontiers in Bioengineering and Biotechnology.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fbioe.2025.1604447). Lab-on-chip study using porcine plasma (n = 2 to 3). Focused ultrasound at 150 kHz cut microclot size by up to three-fold. Caveat: no blood flow, and all three authors work for the company behind the device. No patient has been treated this way. Discussed in: [Microclots treatment](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-treatment/).
- **Investigation of the synergistic effect of enzymatic and ultrasound-induced amyloid microclot degradation, 2025.** *Journal of Thrombosis and Thrombolysis.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11239-025-03220-0). Lab-on-chip study. Without ultrasound, none of three enzymes, nattokinase included, significantly shrank plasma-derived microclots. Caveat: an in vitro result, so it does not show what happens in people. Discussed in: [Microclots treatment](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-treatment/).

## 6. Related conditions: ME/CFS and long COVID

- **Nunes et al., 2022.** *Pharmaceuticals.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph15080931). Pilot study of 25 people with ME/CFS and 15 healthy controls. Mean amyloid signal area was 1.37% against 0.10% in controls, and platelets looked hyperactivated in 80% of ME/CFS samples. Caveat: a pilot, small, self-reported symptom severity, one lab, and the only peer-reviewed ME/CFS microclot study the site found, with no independent replication yet. Discussed in: [Microclots in ME/CFS, POTS and fibromyalgia](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/microclots-me-cfs/).
- **Differentiation of Prior SARS-CoV-2 Infection and Postacute Sequelae by Standard Clinical Laboratory Measurements in the RECOVER Cohort, 2024.** *Annals of Internal Medicine.* [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7326/M24-0737). NIH-funded cohort comparing 25 routine blood and urine tests in nearly 10,000 adults. It found no clinically useful differences between people with and without long COVID. Caveat: it tested routine laboratory values, not microclot staining. Discussed in: [Long COVID blood test](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/long-covid-blood-test/).
- **Long COVID Clinical Guidance for Healthcare Providers, 2026.** U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. [Official page](https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html). Official guidance stating that no laboratory test can definitively diagnose or rule out long COVID. Caveat: guidance, not a study, and it does not address microclot staining specifically. Discussed in: [Long COVID blood test](https://amyloid-test.com/resources/long-covid-blood-test/).

## How this list is maintained

Entries come from the sources cited in articles on amyloid-test.com, and the study type, size and caveat for each are taken from the site article that discusses it. A source is added only when an article describes it specifically. Where a paper has a published critique or reply, both are listed. Corrections are welcome.
